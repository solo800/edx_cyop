The submission for the choose-your-own project will be three files: a report in the form of both a PDF document and Rmd file and the R script that performs your machine learning task. You must also provide access to your dataset, either through automatic download in your script or inclusion in a GitHub repository.

You are **strongly discouraged** from using well-known datasets, particularly ones that have been used as examples in previous courses or are similar to them. You **may not submit the same project** for both the MovieLens and Choose Your Own project submissions and your Choose Your Own project may not simply be a variation of your MovieLens project.

We recommend submitting a link to a GitHub repository with these three files and your dataset. Your grade for the project will be based on your report and your script.

### **Report and Script**

Your report and script will be graded by your peers, based on a rubric defined by the course staff, as well as by the course staff. **The staff grade will be your final grade for the project.** Note that due to the volume of submissions and the number of graders, it can take up to four weeks to receive your staff grade, although we strive for a faster turnaround time\! To receive your grade, you must review and grade the reports of five of your fellow learners after submitting your own. This will give you the chance to learn from your peers. You are encouraged to give your peers thoughful and specific feedback on their projects.

**Please pay attention to the due dates listed\!** The project submission is due before the end of the course to allow time for peer grading. Also note that you must grade the reports of your peers by the course close date in order to receive your grade.

### **Plagiarism**

We consider plagiarism to be taking ideas, text, or code from other people without proper citation. Plagiarism is strictly prohibited. In addition to not committing plagiarism, we expect the core of your submissions to be primarily your own original work. Any borrowed ideas, text, or code must be appropriately cited and only constitute a minority of your project. When incorporating another personâ€™s words, they must be enclosed in quotation marks and the sources must be cited. Similarly, all paraphrased text must be acknowledged and cited. While it is acceptable to use individual lines or small sections of code from others, the majority of the code should be your own work. Use code comments to identify borrowed lines of code and cite the sources.

For more details on plagiarism and how to appropriately acknowledge sources when quoting or paraphrasing, please see this guide: [https://usingsources.fas.harvard.edu/what-constitutes-plagiarism-0](https://usingsources.fas.harvard.edu/what-constitutes-plagiarism-0) Generative artificial intelligence tools, such as ChatGPT or GitHub Copilot, are permitted but should be used sparingly. If utilized, they should augment your original work and constitute only a small portion of your code. Any use of such tools must be acknowledged and cited.
